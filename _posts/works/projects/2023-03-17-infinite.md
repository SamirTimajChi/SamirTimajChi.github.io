## Abstract

*(infinite)* approaches a de-conceptualization of music into free-form sounds independent of time and space. The central concept is the absence of dictatorship and hierarchies of sound. The composer is a mere facilitator; true authorship lies with human-machine assemblages exploring random processing and self-organized structures. Algorithmic randomness serves as a complementary method examining sound from non-human-biased perspectives.

---

## Research Questions

- How does musical composition incorporate rhizomatic movements by working with a non-hierarchical form structured to have an independent large-scale structure?
- How does the machine display its agency?

---

## Method

A **Bösendorfer Imperial Piano** on stage, surrounded by nine microphones and a camera mounted above. A performer in a green suit (chroma key) recorded 3,000+ individual audio and visual recordings using vibrators, screwdrivers, steel balls, ventilators, table tennis balls, and extended piano techniques.

Files are systematically named with parameters (duration, pitch, sound family, average amplitude, position, etc.) stored in a database. Audiences can interact by altering parameters and solving puzzles hidden for discovery.

---

## Structure — The Rhizome

Musical material is algorithmically combined. Performance structure mimics rhizome growth: dominant **stolons** branch out, generating new stolons indefinitely. A stolon can be defined by mode of production, set of strings, loudness, length, or number of performers. Up to ten stolons active simultaneously. Other sound events group randomly around a stolon until one establishes a new stolon — self-replication with variation. Rate of change is driven by a random number generator following patterns.

Results are displayed on a website performing the instrument in real time, with each client receiving fresh output at any moment.

---

## Technology

**Technical stack:** WebAudio API, Three.js, custom database, WebGL, QR code system.

**Interaction design:** passive autonomous state (1–10 layers) → user unlocks deeper layers via clicks/QR codes → each visit generates a unique performance.

Recorded at the **Hybrid New Music Lab, Hochschule für Musik Carl Maria von Weber Dresden**.

---

## Rethinking Technology-Based Music

The choice of Max/MSP, Pure Data, and p5.js as focal tools is predicated on their distinguished positions within the computer music community. Max/MSP — developed at IRCAM in the late 1980s by Miller Puckette and later commercialized by Cycling '74 — resonates with Umberto Eco's concept of the "open work": art open to multiple interpretations, aligning with Stockhausen's *Klavierstücke XI*.
